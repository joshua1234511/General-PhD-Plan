# PhD Publication Pipeline — Shared Template

A **general, shareable** toolkit for getting doctoral research from an idea to a published
journal paper: a stage-by-stage pipeline, a tracker for every paper in flight, a fill-in template
for your programme's publication rules, and an AI-assistant skill that does the heavy lifting at
each stage — with **no personal, institutional or copyrighted content**.

## Why a pipeline

Many doctoral programmes make the degree conditional on published or accepted journal papers,
and journal review cycles routinely take a year or more per venue. A paper that starts in the
final year often misses the deadline. Treating publication as a pipeline — several papers at
different stages, each moving forward every month — is what makes the requirement reachable.

## The pipeline

```
💡 Idea → 🔎 Literature → 📐 Plan → ✍️ Draft → ✅ Citation audit → 🧐 Pre-submission review
      → 🎯 Venue prep → 📤 Submitted → 💬 Under review → 🔁 Revise & respond → 🏁 Accepted
                                              ↘ ❌ Rejected → back to 🎯 with the next venue
```

| # | Stage | What "done" looks like | Skill workflow |
|---|-------|------------------------|----------------|
| 1 | **Idea** | One paragraph: the problem, why it matters, the intended contribution | A — start a paper |
| 2 | **Literature** | A search strategy, a literature matrix, and an evidence card for every source that carries weight | B — literature pass |
| 3 | **Plan** | Research question, contribution, structure and word budget agreed with co-authors | A — paper plan |
| 4 | **Draft** | A complete manuscript, every claim backed by a cited source or your own data | — |
| 5 | **Citation audit** | Every reference exists, resolves, and actually says what the text claims | C — citation integrity check |
| 6 | **Pre-submission review** | A hostile read from several reviewer perspectives, with fixes made | D — pre-submission review |
| 7 | **Venue prep** | Target journal chosen; formatting, cover letter, title page, checklist and AI-use disclosure done | E — venue attempt · G — AI disclosure |
| 8 | **Submitted / under review** | Submission logged with date and manuscript ID | E — venue attempt |
| 9 | **Revise & respond** | Reviewer comments parsed into a roadmap; every point answered in the response letter | F — reviewer response |
| 10 | **Accepted** | Acceptance letter filed; counted against your programme's requirement | — |

## How to use this

1. **Know the target.** Fill in §5 of [`program-requirements.md`](program-requirements.md):
   how many papers, which journal lists or indexes count, authorship position, and whether
   conference papers or cases count. Confirm it with your doctoral office in writing.
2. **Fill the pipeline.** Add every paper idea to [`publication-tracker.md`](publication-tracker.md),
   even early ones. Aim to have papers at several different stages at once.
3. **Install the skill.** Follow [`skills/README.md`](skills/README.md), then ask your assistant
   in plain English: *"new paper on …"*, *"lit scan on …"*, *"check my citations"*,
   *"review this before I submit"*, *"submitting to …"*, *"I got reviewer comments"*.
4. **Review monthly.** Every paper should move at least one stage, or have a written reason why not.

## Choosing a journal

- Check the journal against the **lists your programme accepts** (for example ABDC, ABS/AJG,
  FT50) and the **indexes it requires** (for example Scopus, Web of Science) — a journal can be
  on one and not the other.
- Read recent issues: does the journal publish your kind of paper, method and context?
- Look up its **AI-use policy**, word limits and formatting rules before you draft the final version.
- Watch for predatory journals: unsolicited invitations, fast-acceptance promises and unclear
  peer review are warning signs. Verify the journal on the official list itself, not on the
  journal's own website.

## What is *not* in here — on purpose

- **Anyone's manuscripts, data, reviews or grades.** Keep real paper folders in a private repo
  (the `.gitignore` excludes `research/` and `papers/`).
- **Published papers, licensed journal lists or datasets.** Those belong to their owners.
- **Names of institutions, faculty, staff or students, and contact details.**
