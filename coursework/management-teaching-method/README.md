## Course description
Conducted in **online and offline** (workshop) mode — participants must be able to
attend at IIM Sambalpur campus. Engages participants in a variety of
teaching-learning methods. Various individual and small-group assignments form the
basis for learning. Each segment is evaluated; **no separate examination.**

Two sections:
1. **Input** — sessions conducted by the instructor (≈ 9–10 sessions)
2. **Practice teaching** by participants (≈ 11–12 sessions)

**Input session covers:**
- Introduction to effective classroom communication
- Storytelling as pedagogy
- Organising and conducting sessions for case teaching
- Simulations in management teaching
- Role of feedback in learning and pedagogy
- Participative teaching through the lecture method
- Facilitation skills: theoretical & conceptual foundations of facilitation for participative learning

**Practice teaching:** each participant teaches a **half-hour module** on a topic of
their choice using a **non-lecture method**, assuming the instructor role, then gets
detailed feedback from participants and faculty. Number of practice sessions = half
the number of participants (30 min teaching + 15 min evaluation/discussion each).

## Pedagogy
Participative & experiential: class discussion, presentation, case discussion,
video analysis, simulations, exercises, small projects (outside class), and
teaching a half-hour class using a non-lecture method.

## Objectives
1. Familiarise participants with selected participative pedagogies usable in regular
   management programmes or short-duration executive programmes.
2. Make them aware of the challenges of becoming a skilled **facilitator** managing a
   classroom with participative pedagogies.

## Prerequisites
Readiness to observe, reflect, listen, and participate through reading and class observation.

## Course competencies
1. Listening deeply
2. Reading and conceptualization
3. Readiness for taking different behavioural roles and reflecting about them

## In-class exercise — "Win As Much As You Can"
A cooperation-vs-competition simulation. Four teams form a cluster; over 10 rounds
each team chooses **X** or **Y**; payoff depends on the cluster's pattern:

| Pattern | Payoff |
|---------|--------|
| 4 Xs | Lose Re 1.00 each |
| 3 Xs, 1 Y | Xs win Re 1.00 each · Y loses Rs 3.00 |
| 2 Xs, 2 Ys | Xs win Rs 2.00 each · Ys lose Rs 2.00 each |
| 1 X, 3 Ys | X wins Rs 3.00 · Ys lose Re 1.00 each |
| 4 Ys | Win Re 1.00 each |

Confer with your team before each round; confer with the whole cluster before
rounds 5, 8, 10 (bonus rounds — payoff ×3, ×5, ×10 respectively).

**The lesson:** individually "rational" competitive play (always X) is collectively
destructive — persistent competition with no cluster trust destroys collective value.
Trust and communication in the cluster-confer rounds are where value is created or
lost. Debrief through the experiential-learning cycle:
**Planning → Experience → Outcome → Reflection.**

## Feedback Project (Project on feedback — 20%)
Graded out-of-class exercise. Two conditions with **two different partners** outside
the class — **receiving** negative feedback and **giving** negative feedback (~15–20
min each, private, confidential). Write a report per condition on **9 reflection
dimensions** (process only, not content; partner anonymised), **integrating the
feedback readings**. Bring a copy to the designated class for discussion. See
[notes.md](notes.md) for the full breakdown.

## Readings on file
1. **Gabrieli, Ansel & Krachman (2015)** — *Ready To Be Counted: The Research Case
   for Education Policy Action on Non-Cognitive Skills* (Transforming Education
   working paper, v1.0 Dec 2015). Cognitive vs **non-cognitive** skills;
   non-cognitive split into **intrapersonal** (interest, curiosity, persistence,
   self-control, self-regulation) and **interpersonal** (empathy, listening,
   cooperation). Findings across academics, career, and well-being.
2. **Ben Johnson (2013)** — *Great Teachers Don't Teach* (Edutopia). Constructivism
   (Dewey 1935), experiential / project-based / performance-based learning, inquiry,
   Socratic method; teachers as "learning engineers" who put students in the
   driver's seat.
3. **Lawrence & Wiswell (1995)** — *Feedback Is a Two-Way Street* (*Training &
   Development*). Two-way feedback via **specificity, empathy, inquiry**; listening at
   the centre of empathy.
4. **Grant Wiggins (2012)** — *Seven Keys to Effective Feedback* (*Educational
   Leadership*). Feedback ≠ advice/evaluation; the seven keys (goal-referenced,
   tangible, actionable, user-friendly, timely, ongoing, consistent).
5. **Ludlow & Panton (1996)** — *Some Aspects to Keep in Mind* (giving & receiving
   feedback handout). "Active listening and feedback go hand in hand."
