Section-wise abstracts + questions to ask, so you can skim and be class-ready.

## Books — the craft & style

### Booth, Colomb, Williams et al. — *The Craft of Research*
**Abstract.** The standard end-to-end guide: turning a topic into a *question* into
a *problem*; making claims backed by reasons + evidence; anticipating readers'
objections; drafting and revising.
**Questions:** Have I stated my research *problem* (what's lost if it's unsolved),
not just a topic? Who is my reader and what will they object to?

### Wendy Belcher — *Writing Your Journal Article in Twelve Weeks*
**Abstract.** A structured workflow to move a draft to a submittable journal
article; argument, contribution, matching to a target journal, revision.
**Questions:** What is my article's single core argument in one sentence? Which
ABDC-ranked journal is it *actually* aimed at?

### Becker — *Writing for Social Scientists*
**Abstract.** On the sociology of academic writing: perfectionism, jargon, and the
"one right way" myth; write to think, revise ruthlessly.
**Questions:** Where am I hiding behind jargon instead of saying the plain thing?

### Sword — *Stylish Academic Writing* · Zinsser — *On Writing Well*
**Abstract.** Clarity, concreteness, voice; cutting clutter; that scholarly and
readable aren't opposites.
**Questions:** Can I cut 10% of my last draft with no loss of meaning?

## Articles — publishing in management / IS

### Whetten (1989), *AMR* — What Constitutes a Theoretical Contribution?
**Abstract.** The building blocks of theory — **What / How / Why / Who-Where-When**;
a real contribution changes the Why, not just adds a variable.
**Questions:** Does my work change the *Why*, or just add boxes to an existing model?

### Webster & Watson (2002), *MISQ* — Writing a Literature Review
**Abstract.** How to write a *concept-centric* (not author-centric) review; the
concept matrix; identifying the gap and building toward theory.
**Questions:** Is my literature review concept-centric or just an annotated bibliography?

### Bem — Writing the Empirical Journal Article
**Abstract.** Structure of the empirical paper; "tell it slant" / rewrite history so
the intro frames the findings you actually got.
**Questions:** Does my intro set up exactly the contribution my findings deliver?

### Grant & Pollock (2011) · Sparrowe & Mayer (2011), *AMJ* editorials
**Abstract.** Front-end craft: titles, abstracts, framing the "hook"; grounding
hypotheses/propositions convincingly.
**Questions:** Does my title + abstract make the contribution obvious in 30 seconds?

### Pratt (2009), *AMJ* — Tips on Writing Up Qualitative Research
**Abstract.** No "boilerplate" for qualitative work; show the data-to-theory link;
use power quotes + tables well.
**Questions:** How will I display qualitative evidence so it persuades? *(overlaps
directly with the Qualitative Techniques course — Jonsen 2018, Tracy 2010)*

### Ragins (2012), *AMR* — Reflections on the Craft of Clear Writing
**Abstract.** Editor's plea for clarity and reader empathy in scholarly prose.
**Questions:** Am I writing for the reader or to impress the reviewer?
