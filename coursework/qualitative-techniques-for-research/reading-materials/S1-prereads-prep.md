## 1. Gioia & Pitre (1990) — *Multiparadigm Perspectives on Theory Building*
*Academy of Management Review*, 15(4): 584–602

**Core argument.** There is no universal, paradigm-neutral way to build theory.
Appropriate theory-building depends on the paradigmatic assumptions (ontology,
epistemology, methodology) you bring to a topic. Organizational study has been
dominated by **functionalism**, which produces valuable but incomplete knowledge.

**The framework (Burrell & Morgan, 1979).** A 2×2 on two axes —
subjective↔objective and regulation↔radical change — yields four paradigms:
- **Interpretivist** (subjective/regulation): reality is socially constructed;
  goal = *describe & explain* to understand; inductive; discovery through code analysis.
- **Radical Humanist** (subjective/change): critique reified "deep structures";
  goal = *change consciousness*; disclosure through critical analysis.
- **Radical Structuralist** (objective/change): objective class/economic structures
  as domination; goal = *guide revolutionary change*; liberation through structural analysis.
- **Functionalist** (objective/regulation): search for regularities; goal =
  *predict & control*; deductive hypothesis testing; refinement through causal analysis.

**Running theme = "structure":** structuring (interpretive), deep structure
(radical humanist), class structure (radical structuralist), organizational
structure (functionalist), structuration (transition zone).

**Bridging.** Paradigm boundaries are "blurred transition zones," not hard walls.
Limited bridging is possible — e.g. **structurationism** (Giddens/Barley) bridges
interpretive↔functionalist (structure is both medium and outcome of action).
But **full synthesis is impossible** (incommensurable assumptions/vocabularies).

**Metaparadigm perspective.** Instead of synthesis, view paradigms from a
meta-level → **metatriangulation** *across* theories/paradigms (not just methods
within one). Aim: "complementarity despite disparity." Doctoral training in
multiple paradigms, multiparadigm research teams, parallel studies.

**Critical questions to raise:**
- If paradigms are truly incommensurable, is "metatriangulation" a genuine
  epistemological move, or does the meta-level itself smuggle in one paradigm's
  assumptions (they concede the viewer is always "rooted" somewhere)?
- Decades on, has the field actually achieved multiparadigm work, or has
  functionalism simply been replaced by a dominant *interpretive* template? (Ties
  directly to the Bansal & Corley worry below.)
- **For your own topic:** could it be studied *functionally* (measure X → Y → outcome)
  *or* interpretively (how do actors construct the phenomenon)? Which paradigm is your
  framework really operating in — and are you force-fitting functionalist
  theory-building onto an interpretive phenomenon?

## 2. Bansal & Corley (2011) — *The Coming of Age for Qualitative Research: Embracing the Diversity of Qualitative Methods*
*Academy of Management Journal*, 54(2): 233–237 (From the Editors, written as a dialogue)

**Taking stock.** Qualitative work grew to ~11% of AMJ articles (2001–2010), and
6 of the last 8 Best Article Awards were qualitative — AMJ is a genuine target
journal for it, despite lingering perceptions otherwise.

**Emerging "AMJ style" (and the worry).** Published qualitative papers converge:
consistent structure (intro/lit/methods/findings/discussion), reliance on
**coding**, detailed tables/graphs/diagrams, and **propositions** to show
contribution. The editors *worry* this "methodological conservatism" (Tracy 2010)
stifles the novelty and plurality that make qualitative work valuable.

**What they encourage:** nontraditional data (narratives, photos, artifacts,
nonverbal), new/challenging research questions, varied analyses (esp. longitudinal
/ process, multi-level), and more innovative mixed methods (qual deepening quant,
not just servicing it).

**Two non-negotiables:**
1. **Theoretical contribution** = *engage and advance a scholarly conversation* —
   change/challenge how scholars think, not merely "fill a gap."
2. **Methodological rigor** = **transparency** (rich description, not just method
   listing), **researcher voice / reflexivity** (first-person), and a clear
   **trail of evidence** connecting raw data → theory. No single "boilerplate."

Rejections are usually for **poor craftsmanship**, not fatal flaws.

**Critical questions to raise:**
- The editors champion diversity while admitting they *won't* publish non-coding
  exemplars (to avoid new templates) — is that a genuine opening, or a catch-22
  that keeps the coding template dominant? (Connects to Köhler et al. 2022 on
  templates, S3–4.)
- "Engage a scholarly conversation" vs "fill a gap" — is this a real
  epistemological distinction or a rhetorical/gatekeeping one?
- **For your own topic:** what nontraditional data could your study use? How will you
  make your *researcher voice* visible and reflexive — and where is your trail of
  evidence from raw data to theoretical claims?

## Thread connecting both papers
Gioia & Pitre argue theory-building must fit the paradigm; Bansal & Corley worry
qualitative work has drifted into one dominant (functionalist-mimicking) template
— coding + propositions. Both defend **methodological plurality** against
premature convergence. Good opening question for S1: *"Has qualitative management
research escaped functionalist theory-building, or just relabelled it?"*
