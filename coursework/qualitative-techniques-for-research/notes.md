## Session 1 — Qualitative research, problem identification & a worked exemplar

**Module I (S1–2):** course intro · organizational research · paradigms · theorizing.
Framed through a worked example — **gig-worker psychological well-being on digital
labour platforms** — to show how a study is built from context → theory → method.

### Setting up a study: the moving parts

- **Research context** — where, who, under what circumstances the phenomenon is
  studied. *Example:* gig workers on digital labour platforms (Uber, Swiggy, Zomato).
- **Theoretical underpinning** — the theory used to *explain* the phenomenon, chosen
  to fit the context. Two used in the example:
  - **Self-Determination Theory (SDT)**
  - **Psychological Contract Theory (PCT)**

> **Key distinction:** a genuinely *new phenomenon* vs merely a *new research
> context*. Studying an old phenomenon in a new setting (e.g. burnout, but among
> Swiggy riders) is not automatically a contribution — you need to argue what the
> context *reveals* that theory didn't already predict.

### Hedonic vs. eudaimonic well-being

| Hedonic | Eudaimonic |
|---------|------------|
| Happiness, pleasure | Meaning, purpose |
| Positive emotions | Personal growth |
| Job satisfaction | Self-realization |

- **Psychological well-being (PWB) is dynamic / longitudinal** — study *change over
  time*, not a single snapshot. RQ shape: *"How do gig workers navigate changes in
  well-being over time?"*

### The two theories (in the exemplar)

- **SDT** — three basic psychological needs: **autonomy · competence · relatedness**.
  Explains motivation & well-being under *algorithmic management*.
- **PCT** — the *unwritten* expectations between workers and platforms/organizations.
  - **Dynamic:** expectations evolve; policy changes can create a perceived
    **contract breach** → reduced trust, burnout, lower commitment, reduced well-being.
- Combining SDT + PCT gives a *richer* explanation (needs-based motivation **and** the
  relational expectation lens together).

### Method building blocks introduced

- **Exploratory field-based research** — explore real-world phenomena in *natural
  settings*.
- **Qualitative methodologies** (choose to fit the RQ):
  - **Grounded Theory** — build *new* theory; engine is **constant comparison**.
  - **Phenomenology** — understand *lived experience*.
  - **Ethnography** — understand *culture & shared practices*.
- **Sampling:**
  - **Sample source** — the population participants are recruited from.
  - **Homogeneous sampling** — participants share characteristics (e.g. full-time
    partners, ≥ 2 yrs experience).
- **Triangulation** (credibility via multiple perspectives): **data · method ·
  investigator · theory**.
- **Constant comparison** (core GT technique): continuously compare participant vs
  participant, interview vs interview, incident vs incident → develop categories &
  theory.
- **Gioia methodology** (data structure): **1st-order concepts** (participant language)
  → **2nd-order themes** (researcher interpretation) → **aggregate dimensions**
  (theoretical constructs). *Ties back to the Gioia & Pitre S1 pre-read.*

### Research principles emphasised

- Know your **epistemology**.
- Keep **RQ ↔ methodology ↔ analysis internally consistent**.
- Be authentic, detailed, clear in writing.
- Use exemplary papers as *guidance*, not a mould — **don't force-fit** your study.
- Follow the **target journal's** referencing style.

### Class discussion questions (participation — 20%)

1. Distinguishing a genuinely new *phenomenon* from a new *context*.
2. If PWB is dynamic — advantages vs challenges of longitudinal qualitative research.
3. Does combining SDT + PCT give a richer explanation of gig-worker well-being?
4. Choosing between Grounded Theory / Phenomenology / Ethnography for *evolving* well-being.
5. How triangulation and constant comparison complement each other in theory building.

> **Method question worth raising:** for an *evolving* phenomenon, is a single round of
> semi-structured interviews defensible, or does "dynamic" force a longitudinal /
> two-wave design? → connect this to your own research interest.
