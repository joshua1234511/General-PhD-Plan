Research Paper Manager — How to Use

A Claude Code skill for research students

Share freely

What it does

Takes one paper from a half-formed idea to a submitted manuscript and a reviewer response, leaving a linked Markdown trail in the paper’s own folder.

The thing it exists to prevent is a fabricated or drifted citation reaching a submitted manuscript. Everything else is scaffolding around that.

Workflow Say this You get
🔍 Citation integrity check “check my citations” every reference verified against the publisher record, every claim checked against what the source actually says
💬 Reviewer response “I got reviewer comments” a revision roadmap, then a point-by-point response letter
📄 Start a paper “new paper on X” folder scaffold, strategy note, paper plan
📚 Literature pass “find papers on X”, “lit scan” a literature matrix, evidence cards, a scan report
🎭 Pre-submission review “review this before I submit” 5 reviewer personas plus a devil’s-advocate pass
📤 Venue attempt “submitting to NMJI” venue subfolder, cover letter, submission checklist
⚖️ AI-use disclosure “AI disclosure for X” a disclosure written against that venue’s actual policy

The two worth having even if you use nothing else are citation integrity check and reviewer response.

Install (once, ~1 minute)

  1. Get the research-paper-manager/ folder (or unzip research-paper-manager.zip).
  2. Copy it into your Claude Code skills directory:
    • All your projects: ~/.claude/skills/research-paper-manager/
    • One repo only: <repo>/.claude/skills/research-paper-manager/
  3. Start a new Claude Code session. Done — it auto-registers.

Not using Claude Code? See research-paper-manager-install.md — it covers Cursor, GitHub Copilot and ChatGPT, and you can hand that file straight to an AI assistant and let it do the install for you.

The rules it enforces

These are the point of the skill. It will push back on you.

  1. Gray zone = FAIL. A reference you cannot independently confirm exists does not go in the paper. It is never marked “uncertain”.
  2. No vibe citing. Never assemble a reference out of remembered fragments of two or three real papers. This is the hardest fabrication to detect and the most damaging when caught.
  3. Every claim carries a citation, or is explicitly marked as your own contribution.
  4. Contradicting evidence gets reported, with a comparison of evidence quality — not quietly dropped.
  5. Every paper has an explicit limitations section.
  6. Source tier discipline. Peer-reviewed > preprint > grey literature, never flattened. Grading is relative to the field: a qualitative study meeting its own discipline’s gold standard rates highly.
  7. The search strategy is documented — databases, Boolean strings, date ranges, inclusion and exclusion criteria — so someone else can reproduce it.
  8. Devil’s advocate before submission. One pass whose only job is to find the strongest argument against your paper.

Set up your first paper

Make a research/ folder. Then just say “new paper on ” and it scaffolds the rest: a strategy note, a paper plan, a bibliography, and the folders for evidence, data and figures. Templates for every artefact live in the skill’s templates/ folder if you would rather start one by hand.

Name each folder after the paper, not the venue — a paper usually outlives its first venue, and the skill keeps one subfolder per venue attempt underneath.

Word and PDF

It ships two small Python scripts that turn your Markdown into a styled .docx or a single-file .pdf — for supervisors who want Word, and for journals that want a PDF. They need pandoc installed; the PDF path also needs typst. Both are one brew install away on a Mac.

Handy to know

  • It reads Word, Excel and PDF that you give it. It uploads nothing on its own.
  • Verification means real lookups against publisher records — expect it to be slower and more sceptical than a chatbot. That is the feature.
  • Keep your paper folder private if the work is unpublished.
